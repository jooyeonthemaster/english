Technologists often rely on quantifiable metrics, or proxies, to measure progress, as these provide concrete and manageable inputs for models and decision-making. However, this reliance can create a bias toward what is easily measurable rather than what is truly meaningful. In many cases, complex goals such as human well-being, learning, or fairness cannot be fully captured by simple numerical indicators. As a result, when these proxies become the primary targets of optimization, they may end up disregarding the very objectives they are meant to represent. This phenomenon reflects the principle known as Goodhart's Law, which suggests that once a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. Consequently, an overemphasis on imperfect metrics can lead to the illusion of progress while diverting attention from genuinely valuable outcomes.
