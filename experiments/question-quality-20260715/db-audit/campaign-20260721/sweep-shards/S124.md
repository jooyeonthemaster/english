The sciences do make extensive use of idealizations. The ideal gas law describes the relationship of pressure, volume,
and temperature of gases under conditions that never perfectly obtain. In particular, it makes simplifying assumptions
about the molecules making up gases-for example, that they do not attract or repel one another and do not themselves
take up volume. The molecules of real gases are not like this, but their behavior is nonetheless close enough to ideal
ones that the gas law is useful. Indeed, there are philosophers of science who argue that this is the best way to think
