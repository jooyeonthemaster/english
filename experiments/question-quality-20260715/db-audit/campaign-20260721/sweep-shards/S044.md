Most people understand that a well-developed plot and interesting characters are key elements of a compelling story. But there is another factor of equal importance—symbolism. This is a literary device that involves using a person, object, or situation to represent something else. It adds layers of depth to a story and is an effective way to explore complex, abstract ideas without requiring that the reader have extensive background knowledge of a subject. For instance, in the novel Animal Farm, George Orwell uses symbolism to criticize the Russian Revolution of 1917. The farm itself represents Russian society, while the animals symbolize different social and political classes, with the pigs standing in for the ruling elite and the other animals representing the working class. By employing these symbols, Orwell makes it easier for his readers to understand the complex political and social ideas that he explores in his novel without any need for them to be familiar with events in Russia.

When a person dies, the corpse is wrapped in cloth, and monks spend three to five days performing rituals to help guide the departed soul toward its next destination.
