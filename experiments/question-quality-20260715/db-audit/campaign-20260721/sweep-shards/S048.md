The blue light from your phone isn't ruining your sleep!
For years, we've been told that blue light from our screens is ruining our sleep.
Curious about whether this widely accepted belief was actually justified, I even tried

It is true that blue light can influence the body's circadian rhythm through a light-

phones and laptops is simply too weak to exert a substantial effect. In fact, research
suggests that screen use delays sleep only minimally and often does not significantly
alter the hormones responsible for regulating sleep. Furthermore, much of the early
research that fueled public concern was conducted under highly controlled laboratory
conditions, in which participants were exposed to unusually dim light followed by intense
light stimuli. As such conditions differ markedly from everyday environments, applying
these findings directly to typical screen use can be misleading.
Equally important is the fact that the amount of blue light emitted from digital devices
is negligible compared to that from natural sunlight-roughly equivalent to less than a
minute spent outdoors. This comparison suggests that the issue lies less in the devices
themselves than in broader patterns of light exposure. Accordingly, what matters most is
the overall amount and timing of light exposure throughout the day. Adequate exposure
to bright light, particularly in the morning, helps regulate the body clock and can even
reduce sensitivity to light in the evening. However, modern lifestyles often confine
people indoors under relatively dim lighting, thereby weakening the natural contrast
between day and night.
In addition, it may not be the light emitted from devices but rather the content
consumed on them before bed that interferes with sleep. Interestingly, features such as
auto-dimming filters or blue-blocking glasses may still offer limited benefits-not
because they effectively block light, but because they function as psychological cues.
When the screen color shifts or one puts on such glasses, the brain may begin to
associate these signals with bedtime, much like a conditioned response.
