## 지문
Learning a second language is easiest during early childhood. When children are __(A) exposed__ to two different languages from birth, they generally become native speakers of both. Adults, on the other hand, rarely attain the fluency of a native speaker in a second language. There is, however, some concern that bilingualism can cause problems for children. This is partly due to something __(B) called__ “code-switching,” which occurs when bilingual people mix two languages together. But code-switching can also be viewed as an extra ability that gives bilingual children cognitive advantages over their peers. Unlike other children, bilingual kids have another language __(C) from that__ they can retrieve a word if they can’t remember it in the first. Furthermore, shifting back and forth between languages can make it easier for them __(D) to switch__ between tasks, __(E) leading__ to superior problem-solving skills. So, although it may lead to moments of confusion, bilingualism could ultimately give children an advantage in terms of overall cognitive abilities.
